# Agents write the model, and a person approves every change to it

> The pages of a company's model are written by agents, on a person's request or in a workflow that runs on its own, and no change enters the model until a person who answers for it has approved it; the checks prove a change has the right shape, and only the person approving it says it is true.

## The question

Who writes a company's model, and who lets a change into it. Core's conventions read as though a person types every page, and two critiques of our published schemas, heard on September 29, 2026, read them that way: they found the slugs, the headings and the evidence rules too heavy for the people who run a company, and proposed hooks, draft states and a gentler vocabulary to spare those people the format. It had to be decided then, because each proposal was tooling, and which tooling to build depends on who does the writing.

## Alternatives

| Option | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| People write the pages, and tooling eases the format for them: a hook that names and files each page, a draft state, business words for the pull request | Every rule stays a cost a person pays by hand, and the effort goes into sparing people a format an agent writes without effort. |
| Agents write, and a change from a workflow that runs on its own merges once the checks pass | A check proves a page has the right shape, not that it is true, and a check that asks for evidence can be satisfied by an agent that writes something shaped like evidence. Nobody would answer for what the model then claims. |
| An editing application in front of the files, a form per type for a person to fill in | A second way of writing to build and keep in step with every schema, and the person is still the one writing, only through a different window. |

## Why

The format is exact because agents read it, and an agent writes it just as exactly, from documents a company already has, from a request, or from an event a workflow notices. What only a person can supply is whether a change is true and whether the company means it. Putting the writing with agents and the approving with people spends a person's time on the one part nobody else can do, which is what lets a company keep its model current without us in the room. It is also how our own model is already written: seats draft and implement, and the Owner merges, so the call states what we have run on rather than what we expect to work.

## Consequences

Every change reaches the model through a person's approval, whether a person asked for it or a workflow made it, and nothing merges on the checks alone. Answering is not a change: an answer reads the model and changes nothing, so it still goes out without anyone reading it first. Core's conventions are written for the agent that writes a page and the check that reads it, and where they still say a rule must be one a person can apply by hand, that sentence is ours to revisit. A person may still edit a page by hand, and the format is not shaped around it.

What we are committed to building is the view a person approves from: a change shown as what it does to the company, the objectives it serves, the entities it makes, changes or ends, and the claims whose evidence is thin, rather than as a diff of files. Until that view exists, a person approves by reading the diff, which is the cost the critiques were right about. The call stays right for as long as the person approving actually judges each change; an approval given without reading leaves the model's claims with no one answering for them, and the checks cannot tell the difference.
